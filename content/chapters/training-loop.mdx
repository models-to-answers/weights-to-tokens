## One training step

A batch of examples enters the model in the forward pass. The model produces a
prediction, and a loss function measures how far that prediction is from the
desired result.

Backpropagation follows the recorded computation in reverse to calculate how
each trainable weight contributed to the loss. An optimizer then applies a
small update. Repeating this process across many batches creates the learned
weights used later by inference.

Training therefore stores activations, gradients, and optimizer state that
ordinary inference does not need.

### The model-building pipeline

Pretraining creates broad capability and usually consumes nearly all of the
compute. Supervised tuning teaches instruction-following. Preference training
shapes which answers the model tends to produce. Evaluation decides whether the
result is safe and useful enough to release.

### Turn scale into time and money

Training work grows with parameter count and training tokens. More GPUs reduce
elapsed time only to the extent that communication, input pipelines, and
utilization allow them to. The bill can be estimated; quality still depends on
data and engineering decisions.

### Hand the result from training to serving

A distributed checkpoint is not automatically a release. Training ranks first
write consistent parameter and optimizer state. A publication job selects the
serving weights, converts or shards them into the target format, attaches
configuration and tokenizer assets, verifies integrity, and publishes an
immutable artifact version. Inference workers load that version; they do not
mount a live training process.

### Preference signals are not truth

Human or AI comparisons can train a reward model, but optimizing a proxy can
amplify whatever raters reward—including agreeableness over accuracy.
